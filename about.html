About

Engineering Focus

I work where microstructure meets mechanics, focusing on additively manufactured metal parts, defect-driven performance, heat-treatment response, and fracture behaviour, backed by data tools that turn scattered test results into clear decisions.

Materials & Additive Manufacturing Engineer with a Metallurgy, Material processing, and Machine learning expertise

At McGill's additive manufacturing lab, working closely with GE Advanced Powders & Coatings (AP&C), I lead multi-year projects on laser powder bed fusion of Ti-6Al-4V, IN625, and AlSi7Mg, from operating LPBF printers and handling and characterizing powder to running DOE-based process optimization and taking parts through heat treatment, HIP, and surface and chemical processing. I quantify porosity, inclusions, and defects using SEM/EDS, XRD, X-ray CT, and profilometry, run the full mechanical-testing suite (tensile, fatigue, Charpy impact, bending, compression, hardness, fracture mechanics), and build machine learning models (SVR, gradient boosting, PINN, KNN) that predict fatigue life and reveal what really drives it. Before starting the PhD, I spent years working in heavy industry at ArcelorMittal Dofasco, Algoma Steel, Tallman Technologies, and Hospet Steel, taking on product metallurgy, hot and cold rolling disposition, trials, APQP, and root-cause failure investigations. My path into the field began with an engineering co-op at Larsen & Toubro in India. I hold a Lean Six Sigma Green Belt and an NDT Level II certification, and as a Canadian citizen, I'm happy to relocate for the right opportunity.
